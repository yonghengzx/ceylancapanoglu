  • This website, published at https://yonghengzx.github.io/ceylancapanoglu/;

2. Controller, Processor and Role Allocation

Where we determine the purposes and means of processing — which is the case for this website, for our own applications, for our own business contacts, and for the applications we publish under our own name — CeYlanCapanoLu is the controller (or "business" under California law) of that personal information.

2.2 Our role for client engagements

Where we process personal information on behalf of a client — for example, personal information contained in a client's application, in a client's analytics account, in a client's ad network account, or in a client's back-end services that we administer under the mobile app management service — the client is the controller and CeYlanCapanoLu is the processor. In that capacity we process personal information only on the client's documented instructions, only for the documented purposes, and only for the duration of the engagement.

This allocation is set out in our client agreements and data processing agreements, and it is consistent with Article 28 GDPR, Article 39 of the UK Data Protection Act 2018, and the equivalent processor provisions in the CCPA/CPRA (Cal. 7. Advertising, Monetisation and Third-Party SDKs

7.1 Overview

Our own applications are free to download and are supported by advertising. We integrate advertising and analytics SDKs from the providers listed in Section 8. Each SDK transmits information from your device to that provider. This section explains the rules we apply; Section 8 names every provider and states exactly what each one does.

7.2 The rule: no SDK before consent

No advertising or analytics SDK is initialised, and no personal information is transmitted to any provider, until the applicable consent signal for your region has been obtained and stored. In practice this means:

  • Android: the Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) consent form is shown before any

advertising SDK is initialised. Until you consent, we request only non-personalised (contextual) ads.

  • iOS 14 and later: the App Tracking Transparency (ATT) system prompt is shown before any

tracking takes place. If you decline or dismiss the prompt, our apps continue to work and serve only contextual advertising.

  • EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland: we rely on the IAB Europe Transparency & Consent

Framework (TCF v2.2 and v2.3). The consent string is passed to every mediation partner in the chain, so a single decision governs all of them.

7.3 Consent is broadcast, not per-vendor

Where a provider supports the IAB TCF, the Global Privacy Control signal, the IAB US Privacy String, or Google's Additional Consent signal, we pass that single signal down the mediation chain. You do not have to opt out of thirty providers individually.

7.4 Children

For any application in the Google Play Families programme or the Apple Kids Category, and for any user we determine to be a child, we:

  • Set each provider's child-directed / tag-for-child-directed flag;
  • Request non-personalised advertising only;
  • Disable interest-based advertising, remarketing and lookalike audiences;
  • Disable the app-open and interstitial formats entirely;
  • Exclude the user from attribution profiles used for advertising.

7.5 The four ad formats in our applications

Set out in full in Section 10.

7.6 Your controls

  • In app: every application contains a "Privacy Options" or equivalent entry point that

reopens the consent form so you can change your choice at any time.

  • On device: iOS "Settings → Privacy & Security → Advertising → Limit Ad Tracking";

Android "Google → Ads → Delete advertising ID"; and the equivalent controls on Huawei, Xiaomi and Yandex devices.

  • On the web: Global Privacy Control and the "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information"

link (Section 23).

7.7 "No sale or share"

Under the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act, we do not sell your personal information for money, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioural advertising in the sense of those statutes, except as necessary to deliver an advertisement you have requested in the context in which you requested it. See Section 23.

7.8 Advertising and our clients

Where we act as an ad monetisation consultant for a client, the client's app is the controller and publishes its own privacy policy. We configure the client's consent architecture, ad formats and mediation under that client's policy, and the client is the party responsible for the disclosures in its own app.

10. Advertising Formats Used in Our Apps

Our own applications use exactly four advertising formats. Each format below states its trigger, its user control, and its consent behaviour.

10.1 App-open (splash) advertising

  • Description: A full-screen advertisement displayed when the application launches, before the

user reaches the first screen.

  • Trigger: Shown on a cold start, after the consent flow has completed and the ad unit has

been pre-cached. It is capped at a maximum of one impression per session. It is never shown on the very first launch after installation, which is reserved for onboarding. It is never shown on a resume from background.

  • User control: A clearly labelled "Close" or "Skip" control is present from the first

frame and is tappable immediately. There is no mandatory watch period and no countdown that delays the control. The creative body is not a click target for installation or redirection; an install requires a separate, deliberate action. The control never leads to a landing page.

  • Consent behaviour: No ad request is issued without a valid consent string. In the EEA, the

United Kingdom and Switzerland, only non-personalised (contextual) app-open ads are served unless you have actively consented to personalised advertising. On iOS, declining ATT results in contextual ads only. On Android, declining the UMP consent form results in contextual ads only.

  • Children: Disabled entirely in builds enrolled in the Google Play Families programme or

the Apple Kids Category.

10.2 Rewarded video advertising

  • Description: A skippable video that grants the player a reward — extra lives, currency, a

hint, an upgrade — in exchange for watching it to completion.

  • Trigger: Only ever presented following an explicit, deliberate user action, such as

tapping a clearly labelled "Watch for a hint" or "Double my coins" button. A rewarded ad is never auto-played, never shown on a timer, and never presented without the player's input.

  • User control: A "Close" or "Skip" control is available throughout the video. If the

player closes the video before it completes, no reward is granted and no partial reward is credited. The reward is granted exactly once per completed view, and the granting logic is verified before the reward is applied to the player's balance.

  • Consent behaviour: Requires a valid consent string for the player's region. Where consent

for personalisation has not been obtained, only non-personalised demand is requested.

  • Children: Permitted in Play Families and Kids Category titles only where the platform

expressly permits it, and then only with contextual, non-personalised creative and the age-appropriate treatment described in Section 12.

10.3 Interstitial advertising

  • Description: A full-screen advertisement shown at a natural break in the application flow.
  • Trigger: Shown only at pre-defined transition points — between levels, on leaving a game

session, or on returning to the home screen after a meaningful idle period. It is never shown during active gameplay, in the middle of an action the player must complete, immediately after another ad, or before content the player has actively requested.

  • User control: A "Close" or "Skip" control is available immediately on

appearance, and remains available again after any countdown completes. The application never chains two interstitials back-to-back, and never inserts an interstitial before content the player asked for.

  • Consent behaviour: No ad request is issued without consent. In the EEA, the United Kingdom

and Switzerland, contextual-only delivery applies without consent for personalisation. On iOS, an ATT denial results in contextual ads only.

  • Children: Disabled in Play Families and Kids Category builds.

10.4 Banner advertising

  • Description: A small rectangular advertisement rendered inside the application's layout.
  • Trigger: Rendered in a reserved, non-interactive layout area that never overlays

gameplay input, system controls, buttons or dialogs. It is visually separated from content and never causes layout shift during play.

  • User control: Tapping the creative opens the advertiser's destination in an in-app browser

or the system browser — never inside the game screen itself. The banner is never placed over a control the player needs.

  • Consent behaviour: Consent-gated identically to every other format. Where consent for

personalisation is withdrawn, the banner continues to serve contextual creative.

  • Children: Disabled in Play Families and Kids Category builds.

10.5 Ad personalisation controls

Every application we publish contains a user-facing control that switches the app between personalised and non-personalised advertising. Where you select non-personalised advertising:

  • Advertising identifiers are not transmitted to ad networks;
  • Only contextual, non-targeted creative is requested;
  • Data is not shared for cross-context behavioural advertising;
  • The app continues to show advertising, so the app remains free.

12. Children, Minors and Age Assurance

12.1 Our general position

Our business services and this website are directed at adults and at organisations. Our own applications are directed at a general audience, with a specific subset designed for children. We do not knowingly collect personal information from a child below the applicable consent age without verifiable parental consent.

12.2 COPPA — United States

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA), 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506, and the Federal Trade Commission COPPA Rule, 16 C.F.R. Part 312, restrict the collection of personal information from children under 13 in the United States. We:

  • Do not knowingly collect personal information directly from a child under 13;
  • Do not use a child's personal information for targeted advertising;
  • Do not condition a child's participation on providing more information than necessary;
  • Maintain a reasonable procedure for identifying a child user and obtaining verifiable parental

consent where required;

  • Provide parents and guardians with the ability to review the information collected from their

child, refuse further collection, and request deletion (Section 27).

Where we learn that we have collected personal information from a child under 13 without verifiable parental consent, we delete it as soon as practicable.

12.3 GDPR and the age of digital consent

Under Article 8(1) GDPR, information society services may not be offered to a child where consent is required and the child is below the age of digital consent. The age of digital consent is 16 by default, and Member States may lower it to a minimum of 13. The relevant age in your country therefore ranges between 13 and 16.

Where we offer an app that requires consent and the user is below the applicable age, we require verifiable parental consent and do not offer the service until it is obtained. We apply the same standard to the UK GDPR (age 13, with the Information Commissioner's Office recommending that providers consider a single age of 13 or higher, and not lower).

12.4 Verifiable parental consent flow

Where verifiable parental consent is required, our flow is:

  1. The app detects that it is being used by a user below the applicable age, either through the

age gate on registration, the age rating and content rating information supplied by the app store, or an in-app age self-declaration.

  1. The app presents a parent or guardian verification step — for example a credit-card style

micro-transaction, a verified parent email, or a signed parental declaration, depending on the app and the platform.

  1. We record the verification method, the date, the scope of consent, and the

identifiers of the consenting parent or guardian.

  1. We grant the child an age-restricted, non-personalised experience: contextual advertising only,

no interest-based advertising, no data sharing for cross-context behavioural advertising, and no behavioural profiling for advertising.

  1. The parent or guardian can withdraw consent at any time, and withdrawal stops collection and

deletes the data collected under that consent, subject to the retention periods in Section 19.

We retain parental consent records for the duration of the child's account and for three years afterwards, because they are our evidence of a legal obligation (Section 5.3).

12.5 UK Age-Appropriate Design Code

The Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code), issued by the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) under Section 123 of the Data Protection Act 2018, applies to services likely to be accessed by children in the United Kingdom. Our approach for any service in scope is to assume a general audience unless age assurance establishes otherwise, and to apply the following by default: data minimisation, high privacy by default, no dark patterns, no default public sharing, no behavioural advertising to children, and parental controls where a child account exists.

  • Advertising auction and frequency capping. Ad networks automatically determine which

  • Fraud and abuse detection. We use automated rules and models to detect invalid traffic,

click fraud, ad fraud, and abuse of accounts. These systems do not produce a decision that affects your rights; they cause an ad request to be rejected or a session to be flagged.

  • Content recommendation. Where an application recommends content, the recommendation is

produced automatically from your interactions and is easily overridden.

  • Lookalike modelling in advertising. Some providers build audiences that resemble an existing

audience. These are advertising audiences, not decisions about identifiable people, and they are disabled for children.

26.3 Your rights
